# Concession check — every place the working paper gives ground, and where the film carries it

The brief's third standing rule for Part Three: *"Every concession, every
downgrade, every 'I cannot show this' in those two markdown files must survive
into the film."* This is the list, and where each one lands.

| # | The concession, as the working paper makes it | Where it is in the film |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | The hinge sentence on p. 613 is probably an ordinary technical point about long-horizon budgeting, not a claim about organisations; "cyclical" means temporary. Kit crosses out his own conclusion. | **Ch 29**, animated as an event: he gets excited, rereads, and strikes it through. |
| 2 | What survives is thinner: a reason to go and look, not evidence about what you find. | **Ch 29**, put on screen as the standard the rest of the film is held to. |
| 3 | He withdraws "the bar comes down by less than you would expect" — whether it is true depends on percentage points versus proportions, and both sums are correct. | **Ch 31**, both comparisons animated giving opposite answers; he declines to pick. |
| 4 | He cannot say the policy fails to touch the fear that stops a firm building: wealth effects and demand linkages thin it. So the claim is about how *directly*, not about *whether*. | **Ch 32**, animated as him narrowing the claim in writing. |
| 5 | The good news principle hands QE a mechanism for the very thing he was going to explain another way. His own source damages him. | **Ch 33**, a whole chapter, animated as him writing it against himself. |
| 6 | The hysteresis limb rests on Dixit's "ruined instantly" assumption, which Dixit flags as a simplification; mothballing weakens it. | **Ch 35**, carried explicitly, and also flagged at **ch 10** where the assumption is introduced. |
| 7 | Ch 33's concession means the group of firms that never came back is smaller than he first supposed. | **Ch 35**, second caveat. |
| 8 | Martin and Milas's own "small dose" explanation is complete and needs nothing from Dixit; the two are rivals in principle and cannot be told apart in practice. | **Ch 36**, door one. Left standing. |
| 9 | "Firms thought it was temporary" is the strongest objection, comes out of his own hinge quotation, and none of his three replies is a refutation. | **Ch 36**, door two. Left standing. |
| 10 | QE did not reach SME and household rates; unsecured rates rose in 2008–9; for those borrowers there is nothing left to explain, so the argument is scoped to large firms. | **Ch 36**, door three, animated as the scope shrinking. |
| 11 | The evidence base is limited, uses similar methods, largely originates in central banks, and the authors call their own conclusion tentative. | **Ch 36**, door four, and again in **ch 34** where the verdict is first quoted. |
| 12 | Dixit's own limit: a race defeats waiting, and then the textbook is right. | **Ch 36**, closing beat; first established in **ch 9**. |
| 13 | What is scarce on his account is management *time*, but the tool rations *money*. A critic could conclude he has named the wrong scarce thing. | **Ch 37**, first hole, pointed at by him. |
| 14 | He cannot name an observation that tells his story apart from "any published rule is slow to change". | **Ch 37**, second hole. |
| 15 | A queue ranked by value is reshuffled by a fall in the cost of money, so the next project is not quite rate-independent. | **Ch 37**, third hole. |
| 16 | The defensible version is much smaller: the number moves on the company's clock, not the central bank's. Not *never*. | **Ch 37**, stated on screen as the version he can defend. |
| 17 | Treating a hurdle rate as a rationing device is not his idea; only the use is. | **Ch 37**, final beat. |
| 18 | The claim is not that QE does not work, and not that the theory is false — only that it is incomplete in one place. | **Ch 38**, narrowed on screen one line at a time. |
| 19 | Dixit's half is well-established economics that Kit is mostly reporting; only the second half is his, and he holds it loosely. | **Ch 38**. |

## Deliberately dropped, and why

| The working paper contains | Why it is not in the film |
|---|---|
| The survey of finance directors that started the whole thing, and its authors' own preferred explanation (spare cash). | The brief's Part Three rule 2 excludes those authors. The working paper itself concedes the study cannot support what he ended up arguing, so nothing load-bearing is lost. |
| The account of why a company cannot simply hire more managers, and its source. | Same rule: that author may not be named. The film states the constraint loosens over years without attributing it. |
| The recent study finding that hurdle rates follow the cost of money weakly and over years, known at second hand. | Same rule. Its force is preserved in ch 37 as the rival Kit cannot rule out. |
| The falsification episode: sorting firms by irreversibility, and the correction of his own correction. | Cut for length. It changes no conclusion, and its lesson — that the model is silent rather than hostile — is carried by ch 10's caveat and ch 36's scope. |
| The unresolved question of which bar binds, the queue's or waiting's. | Cut for length; ch 37 already leaves the management half held loosely. |
| The aggregation bridge being narrower than he assumed, because Dixit's industry section is about small interchangeable firms. | Carried in **ch 14** as an on-screen caveat on the aggregation result. |
